about

I read every Bruhn / Niu / ESDU page I could find while my classmates were learning ANSYS, founded a small engineering company called Aeroknacks at 22 that sold a real structural-analysis tool to a real firm, did an MTech at NIT Silchar (9.88/10, but who's counting), and along the way picked up Hindustani classical music and a stubborn opinion that engineering software should be open and stay open. Aeroknacks is on the shelf while I focus on the PhD; the bolted-joint tool that paid the bills is now MIT-licensed on GitHub.

I'm currently a doctoral researcher at City, St George's, University of London, supervised by Dr Sathiskumar Anusuya Ponnusami. PhD wraps June 2026. After that I'm hoping to spend a year on Trueform full-time, hosted at Sathis's CEMS Lab at Queen Mary University of London.

In 2025 I spent some time at EPFL's Computational Solid Mechanics lab with Prof. Jean-François Molinari, working on what became our Engineering Fracture Mechanics review. Closest I've ever come to being starstruck.

things I've shipped

working software > published papers, in my opinion, but I'll let you decide.

  • BJSFM — a bolted-joint stress-field tool I wrote during my MTech, sold under a 2-year commercial licence through Aeroknacks, and recently re-opened on GitHub with the upstream MIT attribution properly restored. The Lekhnitskii / de Jong analytical solution, in plain Python, for anyone who wants it.
  • Geometric Transformations — a MATLAB Live Script for 2D/3D affine transforms, originally written to teach my classmates and apparently still useful.
  • A differentiable phase-field fracture solver — PyTorch-native, runs on a single GPU, ~10× faster than the standard reference codes, validated against four of them. Open-source release queued for the moment the next paper hits arXiv. The technical receipts behind Trueform's inverse-design layer.
  • A small library of classical hand-calculation tools — lug strength, Cozzone plastic-bending, fastener load-transfer, ABD matrices, column buckling. Most still in private repos; rolling them out as Trueform absorbs them.

currently building

Trueform — an AI design partner bound by the rules of mechanics

Describe a part in words, sketch it, photograph a broken one, or just upload your CAD — Trueform builds the geometry with you, runs the classical sizing checks (Bruhn, Niu, ESDU, Lekhnitskii), and emits a manufacturer-ready package: GD&T-annotated drawing, citation-grounded analysis report, and the machine-shop or 3D-printer code. Every number is computed by a validated solver, never invented by the language model.

In active development. Currently pitching it for the O'Shaughnessy Fellowship 2026.
