about

This is my little scrapbook of work, half-finished ideas, and the things I keep coming back to.

I'm a doctoral researcher in computational mechanics at City, St George's, University of London, finishing in June 2026 and working with Dr Sathiskumar Anusuya Ponnusami at his CEMS Lab.

Before the PhD I read a lot of Bruhn and Niu while my classmates were learning ANSYS, did an MTech at NIT Silchar, founded a small engineering company called Aeroknacks that shipped a real structural-analysis tool to a real firm at 24, and started Hindustani classical singing at 3 (I know the ragas, the riyaz is another story). Aeroknacks is on the shelf while I focus on the PhD; the bolted-joint tool that paid the bills is now MIT-licensed on GitHub.

In 2025 I spent some time at EPFL's Computational Solid Mechanics lab with Prof. Jean-François Molinari, working on what became our Engineering Fracture Mechanics review.

things I've shipped

working software > published papers, in my opinion, but I'll let you decide.

  • BJSFM, a bolted-joint stress-field tool I wrote during my MTech, shipped to a company through Aeroknacks, and recently re-opened on GitHub with the upstream MIT attribution properly restored. The Lekhnitskii / de Jong analytical solution, in plain Python, for anyone who wants it.
  • Geometric Transformations, a MATLAB Live Script for 2D/3D affine transforms, originally written to teach my classmates and apparently still useful.
  • A differentiable phase-field fracture solver, PyTorch-native, runs on a single GPU, ~8× faster than the standard reference codes (validated against four of them, after I caught and corrected an earlier inflated speedup claim). Open-source release queued for the moment the next paper hits arXiv. The technical receipts behind Trueform's inverse-design layer.
  • A small library of classical hand-calculation tools, lug strength, Cozzone plastic-bending, fastener load-transfer, ABD matrices, column buckling. Most still in private repos; rolling them out as Trueform absorbs them.

currently building

Trueform, an AI design partner bound by the rules of mechanics

Describe a part in words, sketch it, photograph a broken one, or just upload your CAD, Trueform builds the geometry with you, runs the classical sizing checks (Bruhn, Niu, ESDU, Lekhnitskii), and emits a manufacturer-ready package: GD&T-annotated drawing, citation-grounded analysis report, and the machine-shop or 3D-printer code. Every number is computed by a validated solver, never invented by the language model.

In active development. Currently pitching it for the O'Shaughnessy Fellowship 2026.
