Research profile

Mechanics first. Learning where it earns its place.

I develop matrix-free, differentiable solvers for PDEs at the intersection of scientific computing and machine learning. I am completing my thesis while continuing the validation and development of PhAST. I also author a tutorial series that runs from PyTorch training loops to Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) and Neural Operators.

Before my PhD, I completed an MTech at the National Institute of Technology Silchar and founded Aeroknacks, where I built aerospace structural-analysis automation tools around established hand-calculation methods.

In 2025, I worked with EPFL's Computational Solid Mechanics lab and Prof. Jean-François Molinari. That collaboration contributed to our review of machine learning for fracture mechanics.

Outside the lab, I've practiced Hindustani classical singing since I was 3. The ragas are familiar; the riyaz is the lifelong part.

Research software and engineering tools

Case study 01 · Differentiable simulation

PhAST: matrix-free phase-field fracture in PyTorch

PhAST is a public research codebase for phase-field fracture that combines matrix-free finite-element operations, explicit dynamics, damage solves, and autograd-compatible inverse-analysis examples.

Question
How can fracture simulation remain inspectable while supporting differentiable analysis?
Evidence
Public formulation, benchmark, and inverse-analysis material.
Boundary
Performance and validation claims are scoped to the documented examples.

Case study 02 · Learned assistance

Solver-audited neural proposals for fracture

Presented at WCCM-ECCOMAS 2026, this work tests a narrow role for learning: propose a damage field, then project and audit it; when the proposal fails, retain the exact solver route.

Question
Where can a learned model help without becoming the authority on fracture state?
Method
Proposal, projection, audit, then acceptance or exact fallback.
Result
A transparent feasibility route, with the current timing limitation stated openly.
